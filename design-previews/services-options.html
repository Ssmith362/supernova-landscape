Option 2 · ScrollX Parallax Cards

Sticky parallax stack

Each service is a full split card — photograph one side, copy the other — that pins to the viewport and is overtaken by the next as you scroll. Every service gets a full screen of attention and its summary is always readable. The trade is length: nine cards is a long scroll, so this works best trimmed to the five featured services with the other four as a footer row.

Option 3 · ScrollX Card Tilt

3D tilt cards

A uniform 3×3 grid where each card tracks the cursor in three dimensions — the card rotates toward you, a specular glare follows the pointer across the photograph, and the caption sits forward of the image on its own Z plane so it visibly floats. The most physically tactile of the five, and the most "expensive-feeling" per square inch.

Option 4 · ScrollX Reel / Carousel

Filmstrip reel

Nine tall poster cards on one horizontal track you drag, scroll or arrow through, with snap points. It gives every service identical, generous treatment and turns the section into something you play with rather than scan. It also takes far less vertical space than any grid — useful given how long the homepage already is.

Option 5 · ScrollX Spotlight / Cursor Highlight

Focus spotlight grid

An even 3×3 grid that behaves like a rack of slides on a lightbox: touch any one and the other eight blur, desaturate and recede, while the focused card lifts forward and opens its summary. It is the strongest attention mechanic of the five — the page physically directs the eye to whatever the visitor is considering — and it degrades to a plain, perfectly usable photo grid on touch devices.
